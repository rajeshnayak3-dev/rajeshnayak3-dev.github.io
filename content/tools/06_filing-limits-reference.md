## Limits set by law or program rules

!!! warning "Verify before you rely on a row"
    Program rules change and have exceptions. The **Original Medicare** row was checked against the sources below in October 2026. The other rows are REVEX summaries that haven't yet been re-checked against each program's current manual, so confirm them with the program or its contractor before use.

| Program | Initial claim | Appeal (first level) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original Medicare (Part A and B, all MACs) | 1 calendar year after the date of service ("through" date for institutional span claims); rolls to the next workday if it ends on a weekend or federal holiday | Redetermination: 120 days from receipt of the initial determination (receipt presumed 5 days after the notice date) | Level 2 (QIC reconsideration): 180 days |
| Medicare Advantage | Set by the plan contract (often 90 days to 12 months) | Plan reconsideration: commonly 60–65 days | Check the plan's provider manual |
| Medicaid (fee-for-service) | Set by each state; often 90 days to 12 months | Set by each state | Extra time is often allowed after another payer's EOB |
| Medicaid managed care plans | Set by the plan and state contract | Set by the plan | Check the MCO's provider manual |
| TRICARE | 1 year from date of service (or discharge) | 90 days for a reconsideration in most cases | Check the regional contractor's manual |
| VA Community Care (CCN) | 180 days from date of service (or discharge) | Per the CCN administrator | Requires a valid VA referral |
| CHAMPVA | 1 year from date of service (or discharge) | 1 year from the decision | |
| Federal Employee Program (BCBS) | Generally by December 31 of the year after the service | Per FEP rules, then OPM | |

## Commercial plans

Commercial filing and appeal limits are set by each **provider contract** and vary between plans, states and even products from the same payer. Common ranges are 90 or 180 days for initial claims and 60–180 days for appeals. Don't rely on a generic list; check:

1. the provider's contract or fee schedule exhibit,
2. the payer's provider manual for that product, and
3. the payer portal or a call, with a reference number.

State prompt-pay and filing laws can set minimums for fully insured plans, but self-funded (ERISA) plans follow the plan document.

Sources (Original Medicare): [42 CFR 424.44](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-B/part-424/subpart-C/section-424.44); [Medicare Claims Processing Manual, Chapter 1](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/manuals/downloads/clm104c01.pdf); [Medicare Claims Processing Manual, Chapter 29](https://www.cms.gov/regulations-and-guidance/guidance/manuals/downloads/clm104c29.pdf). !!! mistake "Auditor's red flag"
    Teams that keep one spreadsheet of “payer TFLs” and never update it. Limits change when contracts renew; record the source and date next to every limit you use.
