## What COBRA is

**COBRA** comes from the **Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1985**. It requires most employers with **20 or more employees** to offer employees and their families the option to **continue the same group health plan** for a limited time after they would otherwise lose it.

- The coverage is the **same plan**, same benefits and usually the same member ID.
- The person pays the **full premium** (employee and employer share) plus up to a **2% administrative fee**, so up to **102%**.
- Many states have “mini-COBRA” laws for smaller employers.

## Qualifying events and how long coverage lasts

| Qualifying event | Who can continue | Maximum period |
|---|---|---|
| Job loss (other than gross misconduct) or reduced hours | Employee, spouse, dependent children | 18 months |
| Disability (determined by Social Security within the first 60 days) | Qualified beneficiaries | Up to 29 months |
| Employee's death | Spouse, dependent children | 36 months |
| Divorce or legal separation | Spouse, dependent children | 36 months |
| Employee becomes entitled to Medicare | Spouse, dependent children | 36 months |
| Child no longer a dependent (for example, ages out) | The child | 36 months |

## The timelines that confuse claims

1. **Notice:** the employer and plan administrator must send an election notice, generally within 44 days of the qualifying event.
2. **Election window:** the person has at least **60 days** from the later of the coverage loss date or the notice date to elect COBRA.
3. **First premium:** due within **45 days** of the election.
4. **Ongoing premiums:** monthly, with at least a **30-day grace period**.
5. **Retroactive coverage:** once elected and paid, coverage is effective **back to the day after** the original coverage ended. There's no gap.

So a person can wait several weeks, see a doctor, and then elect COBRA, and the visit is covered.

## What this means for RCM

During the election window, eligibility checks may show:

- **Coverage terminated** (the employer reported the employment ending), or
- **Coverage active but pending COBRA**, or
- Active coverage that later gets **terminated retroactively** if the person doesn't elect or pay.

### Typical claim journeys

| What happens | What you see | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Patient elects and pays after the visit | Claim denied **CO-27** (after coverage terminated), then coverage reinstated | Re-verify eligibility, ask the payer to **reprocess**, or resubmit per payer rules |
| Patient never elects | Coverage stays terminated | Bill any other insurance; otherwise move to patient responsibility per policy |
| Patient stops paying premiums | Coverage terminated back to the last paid month | Claims after that date deny; bill the patient or new insurance |
| Payer pays during the grace period, then coverage ends | Payment **recouped** later | Handle the takeback and rebill correctly |

!!! tip "Ask the right question at registration"
    When a patient says they recently left a job, ask: “Have you received or elected COBRA, or do you have new coverage?” Record the answer and the date. It saves weeks of denial work later.

## COBRA and other coverage

- **COBRA versus a new employer plan:** if the person gets a new job with health coverage, COBRA can end. If they keep both, coordination of benefits rules apply; generally the plan covering the person as an **active employee** pays before COBRA.
- **COBRA and Medicare:** if someone already has COBRA and then becomes entitled to Medicare, COBRA may end for them. If someone has Medicare first and then gets COBRA, **Medicare is generally primary** and COBRA secondary. See [coordination of benefits](/insurance/coordination-of-benefits/).
- **ACA marketplace:** losing job-based coverage triggers a special enrollment period, so many people choose a marketplace plan instead of COBRA.

!!! example "Scenario"
    Daniel's coverage ended 03/31 when he was laid off. He saw an orthopedist on 04/12. The claim denied CO-27 on 05/02. The AR analyst called the payer on 05/20: Daniel elected COBRA on 05/10 and paid on 05/15, and coverage is now active from 04/01. The representative sent the claim back for reprocessing under reference 3345-889. The analyst noted the call and set follow-up for 14 days.

!!! interview "If an interviewer asks “What is COBRA and how does it affect billing?”"
    “COBRA is the federal law that lets employees and dependents continue their employer group health plan after events like job loss, divorce or ageing out, for 18 to 36 months, paying up to 102% of the premium. The person has 60 days to elect and coverage is retroactive, so claims during that window may deny as terminated and then be payable once COBRA is elected and paid. We track those claims and request reprocessing after reinstatement.”
