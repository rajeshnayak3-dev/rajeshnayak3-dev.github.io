## Two different systems

| | TRICARE | VA Community Care |
|---|---|---|
| Run by | Department of Defense (Defense Health Agency) | Department of Veterans Affairs |
| Who's covered | Active-duty service members, retirees, their families and survivors, certain reservists | Eligible veterans the VA refers to community providers |
| Is it insurance? | Yes, a health plan | No, it's the VA buying care it has authorized |
| How claims flow | To regional TRICARE contractors | To the CCN third-party administrator for the region |
| Key requirement | Plan rules (referrals for Prime, authorizations for some services) | **VA referral or authorization before care**, except emergencies |

Military retirees can have **both**: TRICARE as their health plan and VA care for service-connected conditions.

## TRICARE

### Main plans

- **TRICARE Prime:** HMO-like. Enrolees have a primary care manager (PCM), often at a military hospital or clinic, and need **referrals** for specialty care. Active-duty members must use Prime.
- **TRICARE Select:** PPO-like. No referrals for most care; higher cost-sharing; can see any TRICARE-authorized provider.
- **TRICARE For Life (TFL):** for TRICARE-eligible people who have **Medicare Part A and B**. Medicare is primary, TFL secondary.
- **TRICARE Reserve Select, Retired Reserve and Young Adult:** premium-based plans for specific groups.
- **US Family Health Plan:** a Prime option through certain designated providers.

### Who processes claims

TRICARE uses regional contractors in the US (an East and a West region, with contractors that change when contracts are re-awarded, so check the TRICARE website for the current one). TFL claims are processed by a separate contractor. Confirm the region and contractor from the patient's card and eligibility check.

### Order of payment

1. **Other health insurance (OHI)**, such as an employer plan, pays **before** TRICARE. TRICARE is secondary to OHI.
2. **Medicare** pays before TRICARE For Life.
3. **TRICARE pays before Medicaid** (Medicaid is last).
4. Certain supplemental programs and some state victim compensation programs are exceptions.

For an **active-duty service member**, TRICARE is the primary payer for their care.

### Other TRICARE rules to know

- **Timely filing:** claims must be filed within **one year** of the date of service (or discharge for inpatient care).
- Providers can be **network** or **non-network**. Non-network providers can be **participating** (accept TRICARE's allowed amount on a claim) or **non-participating**; non-participating providers may charge up to 15% above the TRICARE allowable amount.
- **Referrals and prior authorizations** are required for many Prime services and some Select services (for example, certain behavioral health, inpatient admissions, some advanced imaging).
- TRICARE generally follows Medicare's payment methods and fee schedules (the CHAMPUS Maximum Allowable Charge, or CMAC).

### Non-availability statements (NAS)
Historically, TRICARE beneficiaries who weren't enrolled in Prime and lived near a military hospital had to get a **non-availability statement** from that hospital before getting certain non-emergency inpatient care from a civilian provider. Laws since the early 2000s removed this requirement for almost all care, and it's rarely seen today. If an older TRICARE claim or a training deck mentions a NAS, check the current TRICARE policy manual rather than assuming it applies.

## CHAMPVA

**CHAMPVA** (Civilian Health and Medical Program of the Department of Veterans Affairs) is a health plan for **family members** of certain veterans, mainly spouses and children of veterans who are permanently and totally disabled from a service-connected condition, or who died from one, and who aren't eligible for TRICARE.

| | CHAMPVA | VA Community Care | TRICARE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who's covered | Eligible veterans' dependents and survivors | Veterans themselves | Service members, retirees and their families |
| Run by | VA | VA | Department of Defense |
| Works like | Insurance: cost-sharing, deductible | VA buys care it has authorized | Insurance plans (Prime, Select, TFL) |
| Claims go to | The VA's CHAMPVA claims processing centre | The CCN third-party administrator for the region | The TRICARE regional contractor |
| Other insurance | CHAMPVA usually pays **after** other health insurance (except Medicaid and certain supplements) | VA pays for authorized care | TRICARE pays after other health insurance |
| Timely filing | Generally 1 year from date of service | 180 days | 1 year |

## VA Community Care Network (CCN)

### How a veteran gets community care

Under the **MISSION Act** (2019), veterans can receive care from community providers when they meet eligibility criteria, for example when the VA can't provide the service, drive time or wait time standards aren't met, or it's in the veteran's best medical interest.

1. The veteran's VA facility decides they're eligible and **creates a referral**.
2. The referral is sent to a **community provider** in the CCN, with a referral or authorization number and an approved scope of care, often called a **standardized episode of care (SEOC)**: the services, visit count and time period approved.
3. The provider delivers care within that scope and sends medical documentation back to the VA.
4. The provider submits the claim to the **CCN third-party administrator (TPA)** for its region. CCN regions are administered by companies such as Optum and TriWest; check the referral for the right one.

### CCN billing rules

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Referral or authorization required | Claims without a valid VA referral or authorization number, or outside its scope or dates, deny |
| Services outside the SEOC | Need a request for additional services before they're performed |
| Timely filing | **180 days** from the date of service (or discharge) |
| Don't bill the veteran | For authorized care, the VA is responsible. The veteran may owe a VA copay, which the VA bills, not the provider |
| Don't bill the veteran's other insurance for authorized care | Submit to the CCN TPA; the VA handles recovery from other insurance for non-service-connected care |
| Emergency care | The VA must be notified within **72 hours** of the start of emergency care for it to be considered |
| Documentation | Medical records must be returned to the VA, usually within set timeframes |

!!! warning "The most common CCN denial"
    Care that was delivered, but not authorized: extra visits beyond the approved count, a different procedure, or care after the referral's end date. Always compare each visit with the referral's scope before billing, and request additional services in time.

## TRICARE versus VA in one example

!!! example "Example"
    Carlos is a military retiree, 58, with TRICARE Select. He also has a service-connected knee injury from his time in the Army.

    - A routine visit to his family doctor is billed to **TRICARE Select**.
    - A knee MRI the VA authorized through a CCN referral for his service-connected condition is billed to the **CCN TPA**, quoting the referral number.
    - When he turns 65 and enrolls in Medicare Part A and B, his TRICARE becomes **TRICARE For Life**: Medicare first, TFL second.

!!! mistake "Auditor's red flag"
    VA community care claims billed for visits beyond the referral's approved count or end date. Compare every visit with the referral before billing.

!!! interview "If an interviewer asks “What's the difference between TRICARE and the VA?”"
    “TRICARE is the Department of Defense health plan for active-duty members, retirees and their families, with plans like Prime, Select and TRICARE For Life, and it pays after other health insurance but before Medicaid. VA Community Care is the Department of Veterans Affairs paying community providers for care it has authorized for eligible veterans. Without a valid VA referral or authorization, CCN claims deny, and the timely filing limit is 180 days.”
