## What “no response” means

A **no-response** claim has been billed, but there's no payment, no denial and no rejection on record. On an aging report, it's simply sitting there. Most teams start follow-up at **30 days** (or sooner for fast payers), since clean electronic claims usually pay within 14–30 days.

## The four answers you'll get

| Status | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Not on file (NOF)** | The payer has no record of the claim | Find out why (below), then resubmit and protect timely filing |
| **In process** | Received and being adjudicated | Get the received date and expected completion; follow up again |
| **Pended / suspended** | Waiting on something (records, COB, review, pricing) | Find what's needed and supply it |
| **Finalised** (paid or denied) | Processed, but the result didn't reach you | Get the details; find the payment or request a copy of the ERA/EOB |

## Claim not on file

### Why it happens
- The claim **rejected** at the clearinghouse or payer and no one worked it.
- It was sent to the **wrong payer ID** or wrong address (common with Blue plans, Medicare Advantage and Medicaid MCOs).
- A **paper claim** was lost or returned as unreadable.
- The payer received it under a **different member ID** or patient name.

### What to do
1. Check the clearinghouse history: accepted, rejected, which payer ID?
2. Ask the payer to search by different identifiers (subscriber ID, DOB, billed amount, date range).
3. Confirm the correct payer ID and claims address.
4. **Resubmit** (electronically where possible) with corrections.
5. If timely filing is at risk, send **proof of timely filing** (acceptance report showing the payer received the original) with the resubmission, or ask the representative to note it.

## Claim in process

Ask:

- What date was the claim received?
- Why is it still in process? Is anything needed from us or the patient?
- When should it finalise?

Then set a follow-up date just after that. If a **clean claim** is past the state **prompt pay** limit (often 30–45 days for fully insured plans), mention it and ask about interest.

## Pended or suspended claims

Common reasons and actions:

| Reason | Action |
|---|---|
| Medical records requested | Confirm what, where and the deadline; send with proof |
| COB or accident questionnaire to the member | Contact the patient to respond |
| Pricing or repricing by a network | Ask for the network's contact or expected date |
| Itemized bill requested (hospital) | Send itemized statement |
| Clinical or prepayment review | Ask the expected timeline; send any requested documents |
| Payer system issue | Get a reference number and escalate if widespread |

## Finalised but not received

If the payer says the claim **paid**, get:

- paid date and amount,
- check number or EFT trace number,
- payee name and address (or bank account last digits for EFT).

Then search posting and unapplied cash. If the payment isn't found, see [paid but not received](/non-denials/paid-not-received/).

If the payer says it **denied**, ask for the reason and denial date, and request the ERA or EOB. Many denials “hide” because the ERA wasn't loaded or the denial wasn't posted.

## Bulk follow-up

For many claims with the same payer:

- Use **276/277 batch status** checks or the payer portal's bulk status tool.
- On calls, many representatives will check several claims per call (ask how many they allow).
- Escalate systemic problems (for example, a payer not processing a whole category) to the payer's provider relations representative with a claim list.

!!! tip "Don't resubmit what's in process"
    Resubmitting a claim that's already on file creates a duplicate denial and can confuse the original's processing. Resubmit only when the payer confirms it's not on file.

!!! interview "If an interviewer asks “What do you do with a no-response claim?”"
    “I check claim status on the portal, IVR or by call. If it's not on file, I check the clearinghouse history for rejections or a wrong payer ID, correct it and resubmit with proof of timely filing if needed. If it's in process or pended, I find out why and supply what's needed, like records or COB from the patient. If it's paid or denied, I get the details and find the payment or ERA.”
