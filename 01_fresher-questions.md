## Basics of US healthcare

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is RCM?</summary>

Revenue cycle management is the end-to-end process a healthcare provider uses to get paid correctly for patient care. It starts with scheduling, registration and eligibility on the front end, moves through coding, charge entry and claim submission, and ends with payment posting, denial management, AR follow-up and patient collections on the back end. [Read more](/start-here/what-is-rcm/).
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>Who are the provider, payer and patient?</summary>

The provider gives the care and bills for it (a doctor, hospital or lab). The payer pays according to the plan and contract (Medicare, Medicaid or a commercial insurer). The patient receives care and pays their share, such as deductible, copay and coinsurance. The patient is linked to a subscriber who holds the policy.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is the difference between Medicare and Medicaid?</summary>

Medicare is a federal program mainly for people 65 and older, and some younger people with disabilities, ESRD or ALS. It isn't income-based. Medicaid is a joint federal-state program for people with limited income and resources, run by each state. When a patient has both, Medicare is primary and Medicaid pays last.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>Explain Medicare Parts A, B, C and D.</summary>

Part A covers inpatient hospital, skilled nursing after a qualifying stay, hospice and some home health. Part B covers doctor visits, outpatient care, labs, imaging, DME and preventive services. Part C is Medicare Advantage, private plans that replace Original Medicare for Part A and B benefits. Part D covers prescription drugs.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is the difference between an HMO and a PPO?</summary>

An HMO needs a primary care provider and referrals for specialists, and covers only in-network care except emergencies. A PPO doesn't need referrals and covers out-of-network care at a higher cost to the patient.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What are deductible, copay and coinsurance?</summary>

The deductible is what the patient pays each year before the plan starts paying for most services. A copay is a fixed amount per visit, such as $30. Coinsurance is a percentage of the allowed amount, such as 20%, after the deductible. On the ERA they're PR-1, PR-3 and PR-2.
</details>

## Claims and forms

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is the difference between CMS-1500 and UB-04?</summary>

The CMS-1500 (837P) is used for professional claims from physicians and suppliers. The UB-04 (837I) is used for institutional claims from hospitals and facilities, with revenue codes and type of bill.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is the difference between ICD-10 and CPT?</summary>

ICD-10-CM codes describe the diagnosis, the reason for the service. CPT codes describe the procedure or service performed. The diagnosis must support the procedure for medical necessity.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is a clean claim?</summary>

A claim with no errors or missing information that the payer can process and pay on first submission.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is the difference between a rejection and a denial?</summary>

A rejection happens before adjudication, at the clearinghouse or payer front end, because of errors like an invalid member ID. The claim never enters the payer's system, so we fix it and send a new claim. A denial happens after adjudication: the payer processed the claim and refused payment, shown on the ERA with CARC and RARC codes. We correct, request reconsideration or appeal.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is a clearinghouse?</summary>

A company between providers and payers that checks claims, converts them to each payer's format and forwards them, returning acceptance and rejection reports.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is timely filing?</summary>

The payer's deadline for receiving a claim, counted from the date of service. Medicare's is 12 months; commercial payers often set 90 or 180 days by contract. Late claims deny with CO-29, and we can only appeal with proof of timely filing.
</details>

## AR and denials

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is AR?</summary>

Accounts receivable is money owed to the provider for billed services. The AR team follows up unpaid and denied claims, checks status, finds the reason and takes action, and we track AR by aging buckets.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What would you do if a claim has no response after 30 days?</summary>

Check status on the portal, IVR or by calling. If it's not on file, check the clearinghouse for rejections or a wrong payer ID, correct and resubmit. If it's in process, find out what's pending and supply it. If it's paid or denied, get the details and act.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What questions do you ask on a denied claim call?</summary>

The denial reason and date, which codes or remarks apply, whether a corrected claim or reconsideration is accepted, the appeal time limit and address or fax, whether it can be reprocessed on the call, and the representative's name and call reference number.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What do CO, PR, OA and PI mean?</summary>

They're group codes. CO is contractual obligation (provider liability, can't bill the patient), PR is patient responsibility, OA is other adjustments and PI is payer-initiated reductions.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is CO-45?</summary>

The contractual adjustment: the charge exceeds the fee schedule or contracted amount. It's written off and never billed to the patient by in-network providers. It isn't a denial.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What does CO-16 mean and how do you work it?</summary>

The claim lacks information or has billing errors. The remark code tells us what's missing, such as N290 for the rendering NPI or M119 for the NDC. We fix that item and send a corrected claim.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is CO-197?</summary>

Authorization absent. I check whether an authorization exists but wasn't on the claim; if so, send a corrected claim. If not, check whether retro-authorization is allowed or appeal for urgent care.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is CO-22?</summary>

The care may be covered by another payer per coordination of benefits. I confirm the order of benefits, bill the primary, and if the payer's record is wrong, have the patient update COB and request reprocessing.
</details>

## Behaviour and fit

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>Why do you want to work in RCM?</summary>

Make it personal and specific: for example, that you like structured problem-solving, that every claim is a small investigation, and that the work has a clear result (a paid claim). Mention that you've studied the cycle end to end and want to grow into a denial or QA role.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>Are you comfortable with night shifts?</summary>

Most Indian RCM teams work US business hours. Be honest about your ability to work them consistently, and mention how you'll manage your routine.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>How would you handle a rude representative on a call?</summary>

Stay calm and polite, focus on the claim facts, ask for the information you need, and if the call isn't productive, end it politely and call back or escalate per process. Always note the call reference.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>What is HIPAA, and why does it matter for you?</summary>

HIPAA sets rules for the privacy and security of protected health information and standard electronic transactions. In my job, I'd use only the minimum necessary PHI, verify identity before sharing information, and never take PHI outside secure systems.
</details>

<details class="qa" markdown="1">
<summary>Take me through a claim from start to finish.</summary>

Use the twelve stages: scheduling and registration, eligibility, authorization, the visit and documentation, coding, charge entry, claim scrubbing and submission, adjudication, payment posting, denial and AR follow-up, patient billing, and reporting. Give one sentence on each and one example of something that goes wrong. Practise this until you can do it in two minutes.
</details>
