## Pick the lightest tool first

| Option | What it is | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Reprocessing on a call** | The representative sends the claim back for reprocessing | The payer clearly made a processing error (wrong fee schedule, missed auth on file) |
| **Corrected claim** | Replacement claim with fixed data | Your billing data was wrong |
| **Reopening** (Medicare) | Correction of a clerical or minor error without a formal appeal | Simple mistakes on Medicare claims, generally within one year |
| **Reconsideration** (commercial) | First-level request to review a decision, often a form or short letter | You disagree, or have new information, and the payer offers this step |
| **Formal appeal** | Written dispute with supporting documents | Medical necessity, coverage, authorization or payer policy disputes |
| **External review** | Independent review outside the payer | After internal appeals are exhausted, for eligible plans |

Appeals take time and analyst effort. If a phone call or a corrected claim fixes it, use that.

## Medicare's five appeal levels

| Level | Name | Decided by | Deadline to file | Decision time (typical) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Redetermination** | MAC (different staff from the original decision) | **120 days** from receipt of the initial determination | 60 days |
| 2 | **Reconsideration** | Qualified Independent Contractor (QIC) | **180 days** from receipt of the redetermination | 60 days |
| 3 | **ALJ hearing** | Administrative Law Judge (OMHA) | 60 days; amount in controversy must meet a yearly minimum | 90 days (often longer) |
| 4 | **Appeals Council review** | Medicare Appeals Council (DAB) | 60 days | 90 days |
| 5 | **Judicial review** | Federal district court | 60 days; higher yearly minimum | Varies |

- Notices are presumed received **five days** after their date.
- Submit **all evidence by level 2** if possible; new evidence at level 3 may be excluded without good cause.
- Medicare Advantage plans have their own process: a **plan reconsideration** first (providers usually have 60–65 days, check the plan), then an **independent review entity** for member appeals, and different rules for contracted versus non-contracted providers.

## Commercial and ERISA appeals

- **Contracted providers:** the contract and provider manual set the steps, deadlines (commonly 60–180 days from the denial) and forms. Often one or two internal levels.
- **Member appeals:** the patient has appeal rights under their plan. Under **ERISA** (self-funded employer plans) and the ACA, members have at least 180 days to appeal a denied claim internally, then a right to **external review** for many medical-judgment denials. Providers can appeal on the member's behalf with a signed **authorized representative** form.
- **State insurance departments** handle complaints about fully insured plans and run external review in many states.

## Peer-to-peer reviews

For medical necessity and level-of-care denials, the treating physician can request a **peer-to-peer** call with the payer's medical director, often before or soon after a denial. Many denials are overturned here without a written appeal. Windows are short (sometimes a few days), so act fast.

## Anatomy of a winning appeal letter

1. **Header:** provider name, NPI, tax ID; patient name, DOB, member ID; payer claim number; date of service; billed amount; denial code and date.
2. **Request in one sentence:** “We request reconsideration of the denial of CPT 72148 for date of service 08/04/2026 and payment under the member's benefits.”
3. **Why the denial is wrong:** the facts, briefly.
4. **The payer's own criteria:** quote the payer's medical policy, LCD or contract section, and show point by point how the record meets each criterion.
5. **Evidence:** list the attachments (office notes with page references, imaging reports, authorization confirmation, proof of timely filing).
6. **Close:** what you want (pay the claim), and contact details.

!!! example "Short appeal letter (fictional)"
    **Re:** Patient Sam Rivera, DOB 04/11/1979, Member ID XJH448190211, Claim 2226…, DOS 08/04/2026, CPT 72148, denied 09/02/2026 CO-50.

    We request reconsideration of the denial of MRI lumbar spine (72148) as not medically necessary.

    Your policy *Imaging of the Spine* (section 3.2) covers lumbar MRI when low back pain with radiculopathy persists after **six weeks** of conservative therapy, or when there are **progressive neurological deficits**.

    The enclosed records show: back pain with left leg radiation since 05/20/2026 (office note 06/02, page 2); **eight weeks** of physical therapy and NSAIDs without improvement (PT discharge note 07/28); and new **left foot dorsiflexion weakness** on exam 08/01 (page 3).

    The MRI met both criteria. Please reprocess the claim for payment.

    Enclosures: office notes 06/02 and 08/01, PT discharge summary, MRI report.

## Tracking appeals

Keep an appeal log (in the billing system or a tracker) with:

- claim, payer, denial code, amount
- appeal level, date sent, method (portal, fax, mail), and proof of submission
- payer's decision deadline and your follow-up date
- outcome and amount recovered

Follow up when the payer's decision time passes. An appeal that's never followed up is a write-off waiting to happen.

## When not to appeal

- The denial is correct and the fix is a corrected claim or billing another payer.
- The claim is past every deadline with no proof of timely filing.
- The cost to appeal is higher than the expected recovery (teams often set small-balance thresholds).
- The service is a contract exclusion the provider agreed to.

!!! interview "If an interviewer asks “What are the levels of Medicare appeals?”"
    “Five levels: redetermination by the MAC within 120 days; reconsideration by a Qualified Independent Contractor within 180 days; an Administrative Law Judge hearing within 60 days if the amount in controversy is met; Medicare Appeals Council review within 60 days; and federal district court review within 60 days, with a higher amount in controversy. Clerical errors can often be fixed faster with a reopening.”
