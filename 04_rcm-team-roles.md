## How RCM work is split

Large RCM companies divide the cycle into specialised teams. In a small practice, one person may do several of these jobs. The names vary between employers, so learn what each job **does** rather than memorising titles.

## Front-end roles

### Patient access or front desk
Schedules appointments, registers patients, scans insurance cards and ID, and collects copays. Usually on-site at the US practice or hospital.

### Eligibility and benefits verification
Confirms coverage is active, checks the plan's benefits (deductible, copay, coinsurance, visit limits) and records them before the visit. Works on payer portals and calls payers. Often offshore.

### Prior authorization specialist
Requests approval from the payer for services that need it (MRI, surgery, specialty drugs), tracks the decision and records the authorization number and its valid dates and units.

## Mid-cycle roles

### Medical coder
Reads clinical documentation and assigns ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes, CPT and HCPCS procedure codes and modifiers. Usually certified (CPC from AAPC, or CCS from AHIMA). Hospital coders also assign ICD-10-PCS inpatient procedure codes and work with DRGs.

### Charge entry
Enters demographics, codes, units, charges and provider details into the practice management or billing system, often from a superbill or coding sheet. Accuracy here prevents many front-end rejections.

### Clinical documentation integrity (CDI)
In hospitals, CDI specialists (often nurses) query doctors to make documentation complete and specific, so coding is accurate.

## Back-end roles

### Claims submission or billing
Releases claims, works clearinghouse edits and rejections, and sends secondary and paper claims.

### Payment posting
Posts insurance payments from ERAs and paper EOBs, and patient payments, to the right account and line. Applies contractual adjustments and moves balances to the next payer or patient. Flags denials and underpayments.

### AR follow-up (AR caller or AR analyst)
Follows up on unpaid claims through portals and calls, finds the reason for non-payment and takes action. The largest team in most offshore RCM companies, and the most common entry-level job. See [AR and AP](/start-here/ar-and-ap/).

### Denial management analyst
Works denied claims, analyses root causes, writes appeals and reports patterns so the front end can prevent them.

### Credit balance and refunds
Reviews accounts with credit balances, decides whether a refund is owed and to whom, and processes refunds or offsets.

### Patient billing and collections
Sends patient statements, answers patient billing questions, sets up payment plans and manages accounts that go to collection agencies.

### Credentialing and provider enrollment
Collects provider documents, completes payer applications (including CAQH ProView for many commercial payers and PECOS for Medicare), tracks effective dates and renewals.

## Quality and leadership roles

### Quality (QA) auditor
Reviews a sample of each analyst's work against the client's standard operating procedures (SOPs) and payer rules. Scores accuracy, classifies errors (for example financial, compliance, procedural or documentation errors), coaches analysts and reports trends. A good auditor knows every stage of the cycle, because a back-end error often starts in the front end.

### Trainer
Runs new-hire training and refreshers when payer rules change, often using errors the QA team found.

### Team lead, supervisor, manager
Allocates worklists, tracks productivity and quality targets, handles client escalations and reports KPIs such as collections, AR days and denial rates.

### Analyst or reporting specialist
Builds reports and dashboards on denials, aging and collections so leaders can see trends.

## Typical career paths

1. **AR caller → senior AR analyst → denial specialist → QA auditor or team lead.** The most common path in offshore RCM.
2. **Payment posting → senior poster → reconciliation or credit balance analyst → QA.**
3. **Charge entry → coding training and certification → medical coder → coding auditor.**
4. **Any back-end role → SME (subject matter expert) for a payer or specialty → trainer → operations manager.**

## Skills every role needs

- **Accuracy over speed.** Productivity targets matter, but one wrong adjustment can cost more than ten claims worked quickly.
- **Clear notes.** Someone else must be able to pick up the claim from your note alone.
- **US healthcare vocabulary.** Payer types, plan types, codes and forms (all on REVEX).
- **Spoken English for calls.** AR callers speak to US payer representatives every day: clear, polite and to the point.
- **Comfort with systems.** Billing systems, payer portals, Excel and clearinghouse tools.
- **HIPAA awareness.** Only access and share the minimum PHI needed for the task.

!!! tip "Fresher's tip"
    In an interview, link your target role to the whole cycle. “As an AR caller, I'll often find that a denial was caused at registration, so I'll document the root cause so it can be fixed at the front end” shows you understand RCM, not just one task.

!!! interview "If an interviewer asks “What does a QA auditor do in RCM?”"
    “A QA auditor reviews a sample of each analyst's work against SOPs, payer guidelines and compliance rules. We check the action taken, the financial impact, the notes and adherence to client instructions, score accuracy, and give feedback. We also look for patterns, such as repeated errors by one payer or process, and work with trainers and leads to fix the cause.”
